Set up in one minute.

This early version isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so your Mac asks you to confirm the first launch. It’s a one-time step.

  1. 1
    Drag to ApplicationsOpen the downloaded zip (your browser may have already unzipped it) and drag Dictora into your Applications folder.
  2. 2
    Control-click, then OpenIn Applications, Control-click (or right-click) Dictora, choose Open, then Open again. Only needed the first time.
  3. 3
    Allow accessSay yes to Microphone and Speech Recognition. Accessibility lets Dictora type into other apps and use the Fn key.
  4. 4
    Press Fn and talkDictora lives in your menu bar. Press Fn in any app and start speaking.
Mac still says it can’t open Dictora?

Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to Dictora. Or paste this into Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Dictora.app

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes. Dictora is completely free. It uses the speech recognition and language model that already ship with your Mac, so there are no API keys, credits or subscriptions behind it.

What do I need?

A Mac running macOS 26 or later. Apple Intelligence gives the best rewriting. If it isn’t available on your Mac, Dictora says so in Settings and falls back to light local cleanup that never changes your meaning.

Does my voice leave my Mac?

Speech recognition and rewriting both use Apple’s on-device frameworks. Dictora has no account and no server of its own.

macOS says it can’t open Dictora.

This early version isn’t notarized by Apple yet. Follow the one-minute setup: Control-click Dictora, choose Open, then Open again. You only do this once. Dictora also asks for Microphone, Speech Recognition and Accessibility access, which it needs to hear you and type into other apps.
